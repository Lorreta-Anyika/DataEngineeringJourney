repetitive tasks such as backing up files, updating software, sending emails, or cleaning up logs are easily a burden. Moreover, manually executing these tasks daily, weekly, or monthly is inefficient and error-prone. 
This is where cron jobs come to the rescue
Cron is a time-based job scheduler in Unix-like operating systems that allows you to automate tasks to run at predefined intervals. 
The crontab (cron table) command is used to create, edit, and manage these scheduled jobs.


Field	Values	Description
Minute	0-59	Minute of the hour (0 = top of the hour)
Hour	0-23	Hour of the day (0 = midnight, 23 = 11 PM)
Day of Month	1-31	Day of the month (1 = 1st, 31 = last day)
Month	1-12 or Jan-Dec	Month of the year
Day of Week	0-7 or Sun-Sat	Day of the week (0 or 7 = Sunday, 6 = Saturday)


Character	Meaning	Example
*	"All values" (wildcard).	* in the "Minute" field = every minute
/	"Step" (interval). Use with * to specify increments.	*/15 in "Minute" = every 15 minutes
-	Range of values.	10-15 in "Hour" = 10 AM to 3 PM
,	List of values.	1,3,5 in "Day of Week" = Mon, Wed, Fri


┌───── minute (0–59)
│ ┌───── hour (0–23)
│ │ ┌───── day of month (1–31)
│ │ │ ┌───── month (1–12)
│ │ │ │ ┌───── day of week (0–7)
│ │ │ │ │
* * * * *

practice examples

1.) 0 8 * * * : 8:00 AM every day
2.) 30 3 * * 1: 3:30 AM every Monday
3.) */10 * * * * : every 10 minutes
4.) 0 0 1 * * : 12 AM every first of each month
